Sessions

A session is one immutable parameter set, played as 28 rounds (4 warm-up and 24 scored, in two blocks). Once the first participant claims a code every parameter that touches the task is locked; to change anything you clone the session and collect under a new id. Every event, round and participant row carries its run_id — the session's id, which the design brief calls a run — so two parameter sets can never be silently pooled.

Create a session

Give it a name and it is created from the saved default parameters, as a draft nobody can enter yet. Everything stays editable on the Parameters screen for as long as it is a draft — nothing locks until the first participant claims a code.

Leave blank to auto-generate a short code. Single word — capital letters and digits only, no spaces or dashes (3–40 chars).

Active sessions

Open a session to check its parameters, watch progress and export its data. Participants join with the session code, or are sent straight in by the Simulation Platform.

Completed sessions

Closed to new entrants and read-only. Export the data before deleting one — deleting a session removes its event log with it.

Where the walk starts. A mapping is drawn by anchoring one randomly-chosen position at a starting value, then walking outwards from it in both directions. These four numbers are that starting value — nothing to do with the positions a participant starts with, which are set under Round structure and the Acceptance filter.

Rounding is always to the nearest integer, applied after the whole path is generated, and the adjacency bound is re-checked afterwards — a mapping that fails is rejected and redrawn, so the instructions stay literally true.

Interface and engagement locked after launch
“Ask the AI” and “Reveal” are the primary outcome of this study, so neither may be easier to press: they are one button style at strict visual parity, side by side, and which is on the left is assigned ONCE per participant and fixed for their whole session. It is block-randomised jointly with the crossover sequence — the enrolment counter and the roster generator both fill all four cells of sequence × order evenly — and stamped on the participant and every row they produce (button_order), so the size of any position effect is reported rather than assumed away. Deliberately never redrawn per round or per decision: ~300 actions with the buttons moving buys mis-clicks (a mis-click here spends the higher cost and destroys the ground truth at that position) and inflates decision latency, which is itself a measure. “Stop and nominate” is never in the swap.
The colour of the cost numeral inside each button — and of nothing else on the screen, so red means “this is what an action costs”. Same hue and saturation, a lightness step, so the price difference is legible at a glance. The numerals sit on a white chip (no red meets 4.5:1 against a saturated button fill), and against white the LIGHTER tint is the lower-contrast one, so the cheaper action takes the lighter value and both stay above 4.5:1. Styling that touches a primary outcome is a treatment parameter, not a theme: it locks with the task and travels with the exported data. The Reveal colour is identical in AI-off rounds, where it stands alone.
Between-round milestone pop-ups (“{left} rounds to go in this half”), one in-round encouragement tip, and a focus prompt when a scored round is about to be closed after almost no searching. Every message is motivational and never informational — none names a position, comments on how the participant is doing, or differs between the AI-on and AI-off arms — and each one is logged as a nudge.
Stopping a scored round having bought this much information or less asks once whether they meant to. 0 = only a completely untouched round. “Stop anyway” is always one click away: a prompt that could not be dismissed would coerce the choice being measured.

Round structure locked after launch

Every scored round is one or the other, so these two must add up to the scored rounds per block. A blank round starts with the whole line unknown. A pre-opened round starts with three or four positions already open, showing their true prizes — that is what makes the geometry at the first decision experimenter-assigned rather than something the participant chose. (The data calls these OPEN and seeded; see the Design notes tab.)

WHERE the pre-opened positions sit, which decides whether the value is more likely to lie beyond them (frontier) or between them (gap). These three must add up to the pre-opened rounds per block.

How many rounds of each kind give the AI its sparse K rather than its dense K. Each pair must add up to that kind's rounds per block.

Participants and assignment

Anonymous participant codes only — no names, no e-mail addresses, no free-text identifiers. A student arriving from the class platform enrols with their university student ID, which is the join key between this study’s data and the platform roster. Left button is which of the two paid buttons — Ask the AI, or Reveal — sat on the left of their screen, assigned once at enrolment and fixed for the whole session (the covariate button_order). Round is the scored rounds they have finished out of the scored rounds this session assigns them; warm-ups are not counted, and 100% with a status of started is a real state — the survey and the debrief come after the last round. Status is read from their own session record, so it turns to completed the moment they reach the end. Click any heading to sort by that column, and again to reverse it.

Live monitor

Counters update live from the participant records. Six of the health checks are computed from the event log instead — every decision of every round, far heavier than those records — so they are not fetched until you press ↻ Refresh health checks. Each check carries a threshold; when one is crossed the row turns amber, shows a ⚠, and states the rule that tripped it.

What the AI knows, what a pre-opened round is and why there are three layouts — with the numbers computed from this session’s own frozen pool, not from the design document. Open a session on the Sessions screen to fill them in.

Wording

Everything the study says to a participant, in the order they meet it — the consent screen, both sets of instructions, both quick checks with their answers, all twenty-four survey questions, the debrief and the thank-you. The text is shown exactly as it will appear, with this session’s own numbers already filled in. Edit any of it to change the wording for this session only; the study’s defaults are untouched and every other session keeps them.

Not here: the game screen’s own buttons and labels (“Stop and nominate position 42”, the running cost panel) and the reminder box above each quick check, which restates the rules from the numbers themselves. Those are part of the interface rather than the study text, and they follow the parameters automatically.

Wording only. How many answers a question has, which one is correct, and the order of the questions are fixed — the exported workbook is built from those, so changing them here would invalidate this session’s own data. Wording stays editable even after a session is locked, because it is not part of the design.

Changes are kept on this screen until you press Save session on the Parameters screen — the same Save as everything else.
