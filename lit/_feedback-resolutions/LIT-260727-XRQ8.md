The paper you flagged (“Search Duration”, Marketing Science 2020) now shows its real abstract — the text you saw had the article page's navigation (“Back to Top … Related Information Cited by”) and a list of citing articles appended after the abstract by the crawler that collects full abstracts from the journal pages.

As requested, every abstract in the database was checked for the same problem. Around 500 abstracts (mostly Marketing Science) carried the same appended page-navigation junk and were trimmed back to their real text, and about 11,000 more — across QJE, the Review of Economic Studies, the Economic Journal, JPE, JCR, The Accounting Review and others — turned out to be scraped page headers served in place of an abstract by an upstream data source, on items that had no abstract to begin with; those were removed and are being re-resolved from proper sources.

To make sure this cannot happen again, every pipeline that ingests an abstract (the daily builds, the incremental harvests, the page crawler and all the API backfills) now runs it through a guard that cuts page-navigation text and rejects page-scrape content that isn't abstract prose, and the crawlers heal their caches on every run so old contaminated data can never be re-applied.
