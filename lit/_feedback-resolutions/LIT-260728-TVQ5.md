Paper titles no longer shout. The papers you flagged (the older PNAS ones — "MINIMIZATION OF SYSTEM COSTS IN TERMS OF SUBSYSTEM COSTS", "MARKET EQUILIBRIUM", "THE WORD PROBLEM" …) were registered by their publishers with the title in full capitals, and the catalog was showing it exactly as deposited.

Every all-capitals title in the database — about 17,400 of them, across the INFORMS journals, PNAS, the FT50 catalog and the ABS shards, with the Journal of Finance, The Accounting Review, the Academy of Management Journal, the International Economic Review and Economic Inquiry the heaviest offenders — has been rewritten to read as a sentence: first letter capitalised, the rest lower case, and the first word after a colon capitalised too, exactly as you described.

Restoring the case carefully was the interesting part, because an all-capitals string gives no clue which words are meant to keep their capitals. Acronyms (DNA, RNA, CEO, CAPM, ANOVA), proper nouns (Bayesian, Cournot, Drosophila, Japan, Dearing) and multi-word names (United States, World Trade Organization, New York Stock Exchange) are all preserved, along with codes and Roman numerals — "The structural unity of the DNA of T2 bacteriophage", "Index to volume XXXVI", "A simplified proof of von Neumann's coordinatization theorem", "Infectious nucleic acids of E. coli bacteriophages, IX. Sedimentation constants". The evidence for which words those are is mined from the ~715,000 properly-cased titles and ~306,000 abstracts the catalog already holds, rather than guessed.

Titles that were published in Title Case — like "Modeling First: Rethinking Undergraduate Operations Management with AI" — are deliberately left exactly as they are. Only genuinely all-capitals titles are touched.

One thing this also fixed that isn't visible on the card: the BibTeX export protects every capital letter with braces, so an all-capitals title used to export as `M{A}{R}{K}{E}{T} {E}{Q}{U}{I}{L}{I}{B}{R}{I}{U}{M}` — unusable in a .bib file. Those citations now come out clean, with the braces protecting only the words that genuinely need them. The corrected titles flow through everywhere the old ones did: the citation panels ("Citing references in this catalog" / "Cited by"), the Data Analytics tables, the Google Scholar links and the e-mail alerts.

Every pipeline now applies the same check as papers arrive, so a newly-deposited all-capitals title is fixed on the way in and this cannot creep back.
